# EdictPrincipal: attributable identity on the wire

Status: Accepted

Edict adds a **principal** to the message: the actor on whose authority a Command was issued or an Event raised. It is a durable field on `EdictCommand` and `EdictEvent`, stamped once at the originating `SendAsync` from an edge resolver and carried unchanged through the whole consequential Command/Event/schedule chain. This is the field ALCOA's *Attributable* requires and the one thing the audit log (ADR-0064) could not satisfy without a wire-shape change, and it **supersedes the message-shape clause of ADR-0006/0046** by adding a framework-managed field to the two consumer-facing base records. Today `EdictCommand` carries `CommandId` and `CorrelationId` and nothing else; the principal joins them.

The principal is an **opaque, consumer-supplied `string`**, not a `Guid`. A B2B `tid` is a GUID, but an Entra External ID custom claim, a Keycloak realm, an `oid`/`sub`, an employee id, or a service-principal name is routinely an arbitrary slug, and pinning the type to `Guid` would quietly exclude a chunk of viable identity providers from a framework whose whole promise here is "generic regardless of the auth story." Unlike the route key, the principal is never a grain key and never needs GUID-shaped indexing (it is an attribution field and a secondary index on the audit store), so there is no internal pressure pulling the other way. **Edict imposes no format validation** on the value: what a valid principal looks like is IdP-specific and therefore the consumer's domain. The corollary that *is* Edict's job is that its own audit-store providers must handle an arbitrary string safely (parameterized values on Postgres, encoded/escaped if it ever lands in a key/path position on Azure) rather than trusting it. The Sample app demonstrates consumer-side validation as the example of good practice.

The carry mechanism is the same one the multi-tenant identity theorycraft designs for the tenant id, and the two are deliberately co-located: an edge resolver `Func<EdictPrincipal?>` registered by `AddEdictAudit(resolver)` yields the principal at the originating send; a **grain-entry relay**, sited beside the existing trace-context capture (`ActivityExtensions.CaptureToRequestContext`), reads the principal off the inbound message into `RequestContext` for the turn and re-stamps it onto internal sends (saga `Dispatch`, schedule fire, outbox `SendCommand`). The principal is a *durable field*, not ambient state, precisely because the outbox `SendCommand` effect and `EdictSchedule` fires dispatch commands after a crash or reactivation, across boundaries `RequestContext` cannot survive; `RequestContext` is the per-turn relay only, re-seeded from the durable field at every grain entry. Schedule fires inherit the **arming principal** via `ScheduleEntry` arm-context, the same slot that already persists trace context, so a recurring job stays attributed to whoever armed it. We chose **not** to build a generic multi-dimension abstraction for a tenant feature that is not yet scheduled: the principal is the first occupant of this seam, and the tenant id is a documented future second occupant at the same relay site. **The tenant-id-type reconciliation is owed to that future tenant ADR, not settled here** — a string principal and a (possibly `Guid`) tenant id can legitimately differ in shape because one is pure attribution and the other is a partition/grain-key participant.

Failure is **closed at the origin only**: when auditing is on and a consumer origin send resolves no principal, the framework throws a typed `Edict*` at the edge, synchronously, before anything is persisted, because silently recording "nobody" *is* the breach. This does not apply to framework-relayed sends (saga `Dispatch`, schedule fire, outbox `SendCommand`), which inherit the principal from the relay or arm-context and must never be flagged by the analyzer or fail closed. Edict ships **no `System` sentinel**: a consumer doing non-user-initiated work mints their own principal (`EdictPrincipal.Of("system")` or whatever they choose) and either supplies it via the resolver or through the explicit `SendAsync(command, principal)` escape hatch for context-free origins (admin scripts, imports, workers, tests). The Sample app, which has no auth, demonstrates both: a chosen demo principal at the mock edge, and a consumer-minted system principal for background work.

The wire change is **additive, not breaking** — a new field on the base records and new `Edict*` types, with nothing removed or re-signatured — taken cleanly pre-v1 with no released consumers; the requirement that travels is regenerating the Verify wire-shape snapshots (ADR-0007) so the change is a reviewed diff. `Edict.Contracts` stays free of any IdP SDK and any `ClaimsPrincipal`: the seam is a `Func`, and an ASP.NET adapter that reads a `ClaimsPrincipal` claim is a thin optional helper, never a contract dependency. The **data subject** (the person the data is *about*, distinct from the actor) is deliberately **not** modelled here: it is load-bearing only for erasure, which ADR-0064 defers, and it is domain knowledge the edge cannot resolve from an authenticated claim. Conflating principal with data subject is the most common modelling error in this space; keeping `EdictPrincipal` strictly the actor is the guard against it.
